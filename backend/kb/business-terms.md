# Business terms in plain language

Revenue (sales) is the total money received from selling goods or services before any costs are taken out.

Gross margin is revenue minus the direct cost of what you sold, shown as a percentage of revenue. A higher gross margin means more of each rupee of sales is left to cover rent, salaries and other expenses.

Net profit is what remains after all expenses, including rent, salaries, interest and taxes.

Average order value is total revenue divided by the number of orders or invoices. Raising it is often easier than finding new customers.

Inventory turnover shows how many times you sell and replace your stock in a period. Slow turnover means money is stuck in unsold goods.

Reorder point is the stock level at which you should place a new order so you do not run out before it arrives. It depends on how fast the item sells and how long the supplier takes.

Working capital is the money available to run day-to-day business: cash plus money customers owe you, minus bills you owe suppliers.

Month-over-month (MoM) growth compares one month with the month before it. Year-over-year (YoY) growth compares a month with the same month last year, which is fairer for seasonal businesses.

Seasonality means a regular pattern that repeats each year, such as higher sales around festivals.

Concentration risk means depending on a few products or customers for most of your sales. If one of them weakens, your total falls sharply.
